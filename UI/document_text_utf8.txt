2.5 â€” Code of Ethics
[ SECTION: Professional Standards ]
Medical writing carries significant responsibility. The documents and communications created by medical writers directly influence healthcare decisions, regulatory approvals, patient safety, and scientific understanding. At MedWrite Global Solutions, we hold all members to a rigorous Code of Ethics that reflects the highest standards of the profession.
Core Ethical Principles
Accuracy: All medical communications must be based on accurate, complete, and scientifically sound information.
Transparency: Authors&apos; and medical writers&apos; contributions must be clearly disclosed. Ghost-writing without acknowledgement is not condoned.
Independence: Medical writers must maintain scientific objectivity and independence from undue commercial influence.
Confidentiality: Members must protect the confidentiality of client data, patient information, and proprietary information at all times.
Accountability: Members are accountable for the quality and integrity of their work and must comply with applicable laws, regulations, and guidelines.
Continuous Learning: Members are committed to ongoing professional development to ensure their knowledge and skills remain current.

3.1 â€” Regulatory Writing
[ SECTION: Service Area 1 ]
Regulatory medical writers specialise in the creation of complex documentation required by global regulatory authorities such as the US FDA, EMA, PMDA, and others. This is one of the most technically demanding areas of medical writing, requiring an in-depth understanding of drug development, clinical trial methodology, and regulatory requirements.
Document Types Covered
Clinical Study Reports (CSRs): Comprehensive reports summarising the design, conduct, and results of clinical trials, prepared to ICH E3 standards.
Common Technical Documents (CTDs): Integrated regulatory submissions for global marketing authorisation applications.
Investigational Medicinal Product Dossiers (IMPDs): Documentation supporting clinical trial authorisation applications.
Investigator&apos;s Brochures (IBs): Summaries of clinical and non-clinical data relevant to the study of an investigational product in human subjects.
Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) / Package Inserts: Prescribing information documents for approved medicinal products.
Patient Information Leaflets (PILs): Clear, accurate, and accessible written information for patients.
Regulatory Responses &amp; Queries: Written responses to questions from regulatory authorities during the review process.
Pharmacovigilance Documents: Periodic Safety Update Reports (PSURs), Risk Management Plans (RMPs), and related safety documentation.

Advisory Board Documentation: Meeting materials, briefing documents, pre-read packs, and reports for medical advisory boards.
Medical Affairs Writing: Medical information letters, clinical summaries, and field medical team materials.
Health Economics &amp; Outcomes Research (HEOR): Manuscripts, posters, and dossiers supporting value and reimbursement submissions.

6.1 â€” Getting Into Medical Writing
[ SECTION: Career Entry Programme ]
Medical writing is one of the most rewarding careers for scientifically trained individuals who have a talent for clear communication. It offers excellent earning potential, intellectual variety, the opportunity to work across diverse therapy areas, and the flexibility of both in-house and freelance career models.
What Does a Medical Writer Do?
Medical writers create a wide range of written documents that communicate scientific and clinical information to various audiences. Depending on their specialism, they may write clinical study reports, regulatory submissions, journal manuscripts, patient information leaflets, educational materials, congress posters, advisory board documents, or digital health content.
What Qualifications Do I Need?
Most medical writers hold at least an undergraduate degree in a science, health, or life sciences discipline. Many have postgraduate qualifications (MSc, PhD, PharmD, MD) and prior research experience. However, the most important attributes are strong written communication skills, scientific rigour, attention to detail, and the ability to translate complex information clearly and accurately. Several members have successfully transitioned into medical writing from clinical practice, academia, nursing, pharmacy, and other health sciences backgrounds.
Types of Medical Writing Roles
Regulatory Medical Writer: Specialises in documentation for regulatory submissions and drug approval processes.
Publications Medical Writer: Focuses on scientific manuscripts, abstracts, and posters for peer-reviewed publication.
Medical Communications Writer: Creates content for healthcare professional audiences in a commercial communications context.
Medical Education Writer: Develops CME/CPD programmes and educational content for HCPs and patients.
Pharmacovigilance Writer: Specialises in safety documentation and pharmacovigilance submissions.
Freelance Medical Writer: Works independently across multiple client organisations on a project or retainer basis.

[ SECTION: Editorial Guidelines ]
Professional: All copy must be authoritative, well-structured, and grounded in industry knowledge.

Global: Avoid overly regional references. Use international spelling conventions (British English recommended unless market is US-only).
